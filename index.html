02 — THREE DIMENSIONS OF FLOW

In 0DTE, one metric alone never tells the full story.

Volume counts because high gamma makes every contract a hedging event. Delta Flow (|Δ| × Contracts) captures how much of that volume actually moves the market — it weights each trade by its real directional impact. Notional ($) shows capital commitment. All three are computed in real time, selectable per-metric, and displayed with matching Rolling Peaks for Call and Put against the Spot curve.

TECHNICAL SPECIFICATIONS & FAQ

Which broker and market data feed does GEXPIT use?

GEXPIT is engineered specifically for Interactive Brokers (IB Gateway). It directly captures the official OPRA SPX options tick stream through the IBKR API. You utilize the standard, low-cost market data subscription you already own with Interactive Brokers, with zero expensive third-party data subscriptions or vendor markups.

How does local data storage work and how do I build a personal historical database?

Every tick, trade execution, and computed Greeks surface is logged directly on your local machine in high-performance Columnar Apache Parquet format with Write-Ahead Log (WAL) architecture. Unlike cloud-locked web platforms, you hold 100% ownership of your raw and processed data. You build a valuable, private institutional tick archive day after day with zero recurring data archive fees, ready for Python quantitative research (pandas/numpy/scipy) or automated strategies in Multichart and TradeStation EasyLanguage.

What are the minimum system requirements to run the GEXPIT terminal smoothly?

Because GEXPIT renders real-time OLED heatmaps at 60 FPS and processes hundreds of thousands of ticks intraday via a Numba JIT-compiled engine, a standard modern PC is required. We recommend at least 8 GB of RAM (to comfortably run GEXPIT alongside Interactive Brokers TWS without bottlenecking memory) and a dedicated GPU with at least 4 GB of VRAM to guarantee zero-latency hardware-accelerated rendering on the charts.

How does the Replay Engine and the Intraday Live Rewind function?

GEXPIT features a dual-mode high-throughput replay engine: you can perform Full-Session Replays on any previously recorded day at variable speeds (1x to 300x) with zero data loss. Furthermore, during live market sessions, you can rewind the timeline to re-examine morning Initial Balance breakouts, heavy block sweeps, or key turning points, and then instantly return to live streaming with a single click (1-Click Live Snap) without missing newly arriving ticks.

How does the Flow Heatmap work and what are the display modes?

The heatmap renders Call and Put volume across the SPX strike chain in real time on a pure OLED black background, synchronized with the Rolling Volume Profile at the selected timeframe. It includes 10 display modes in two categories:

— GAMMA (GEX) —

  • GEX Linear: Proportional cumulative exposure across active strikes.
  • GEX Logarithmic: Amplifies far-OTM activity and tail positioning.
  • GEX Rolling (30m): Short-window view of recent intraday shifts.

— ORDER FLOW & VOLUMES —

  • Volume Flow: Strike-level volume from IBKR, synced with Rolling Profile timeframe.
  • Smooth Flow: Two-color continuous gradient on net directional volume.
  • Dominance: Each strike colored by dominant side (Call or Put) with intensity on gross volume — ATM Straddle nodes glow at maximum brightness.
  • Delta Dominance: Dominance weighted by |Δ| × Volume, emphasizing the real hedging risk of each strike rather than raw premium.
  • Call Only / Put Only: Mono-channel views — deep OLED blacks on inactive strikes, pure green or red on active ones.
  • Dual Panels: Split display — Calls above, Puts below, synchronized on the same spot axis.
How do Multi-Timeframe Volume Profiles, Flow filters and Market Regime work?

Tab FLOWS HEATMAP lets you filter the Rolling Volume Profile and separate Call Flow & Put Flow across seven timeframes: 1 min, 5 min, 10 min, 15 min, 30 min, 60 min, and 120 min — as well as the full cumulative session. The same timeframe window controls the heatmap in real time.

The HUD also tracks the Initial Balance (09:30–10:00 NY) with exact breakout metrics. If the extractor starts late, a fallback window based on the first 30 minutes of actual data activates automatically, with an advisory displayed in the HUD.

Tab 1 classifies the session into one of four Market Regimes derived from the relative position of Spot, Zero Gamma and Vol Trigger:
Volatility Damping (Long Gamma, range compression) — Volatility Expansion (Long Gamma, range extension) — Delta Squeeze (Short Gamma, dealer velocity high) — Volatility Cascade (Short Gamma, pro-cyclical momentum).

What is CVD, how does Lee-Ready classification work, and what are Sweep Alarms?

Each trade is classified with the Lee-Ready algorithm using a 250ms quote midpoint window. Trades above the midpoint are Buy, below are Sell, and at-midpoint trades are split 50/50 — preserving total volume without introducing artificial directional bias.

Three parallel metrics are available: Notional ($), Volume (contracts), and Delta Flow (|Δ| × Volume) — which captures directional hedging urgency independent of premium level. Rolling peaks for Call and Put separately are shown against the Spot curve with exact time windows. Sweep Alarms fire in real time above configurable dollar thresholds.

Raw contract count is misleading: 2,000 deep OTM contracts at $0.05 represent $10,000, while 2,000 ATM contracts at $15.00 represent $3,000,000 and impose massive hedging obligations on dealers. GEXPIT's Monetary CVD weights every execution by actual dollar notional (Contracts × Price × 100) rather than share count.

How are Greeks (GEX, VEX, DEX, CEX) and multi-strike exposures calculated in real time?

Tab GEX VEX DEX CEX shows five synchronized vertical profiles by strike: Total Volume, GEX (Gamma Dollar Hedging $/pt), VEX (Vanna — ∂Δ/∂σ), DEX (Net Dollar Delta), and CEX (Charm — ∂Δ/∂t). All five panels are computed by a Numba JIT Black-Scholes engine and displayed as stacked Call/Put horizontal bars with three-level density color grading: LVN (low liquidity, grey), Neutral, and HVN (high density, full saturation). A toolbar controls the time window (1m to full session) and bar layout (stacked or split-centered). All five panels stay synchronized on the same strike axis.

How does GEX Dinamico differ from the standard Net GEX profile?

The standard Net GEX and Total GEX profiles accumulate dealer exposure from historical tick flow — each trade deposits its incremental GEX contribution as it arrives. The profile reflects everything that has happened in the session up to the current point in time.

GEX Dinamico takes a different approach: it uses the total volume traded today per strike and recalculates the full gamma exposure using the current spot price and implied volatility via the Black-Scholes formula. When SPX moves, the entire profile is recalculated — walls shift, Zero Gamma migrates, and the Vol Trigger updates. This is how the market actually re-hedges. The Vol Trigger in Dinamico mode is found with a price-space scan: the engine re-evaluates total exposure at hypothetical spot levels (±60pt grid, 5pt step) and finds the exact price where net dealer exposure crosses zero — not a crossing along the strike chain.

Historical shift markers (1m, 5m, 15m, 30m, 60m and 120m ago) remain available in the standard modes so you can see how walls have migrated during the session. Finally, the embedded Velocity Profile (q4) maps how rapidly dealers are absorbing or pushing the price at key levels in real time.

What is the 0DTE Expected Move Corridor and how does Straddle Decay work?

Tab EM DECAY shows two panels. The upper panel is the 0DTE Expected Move Corridor: it plots the SPX Spot price against static ±1SD, ±2SD, and ±3SD bands computed from the ATM straddle price at the open (certified from the first clean tick at 09:30 NY). Two additional dynamic bands at ±1SD and ±2SD update every tick as ATM implied volatility changes intraday.

The lower panel shows the Straddle ATM Decay Corridor in SPX points. A theoretical decay curve tracks how much the ATM straddle should be worth as time passes through the 390-minute session — proportional of remaining time. A second live curve plots the actual straddle price every tick. When the live price diverges from the theoretical decay, it signals that IV is expanding or compressing relative to what a pure Theta decay would predict.

How does Big Trades detection and Strike Proximity filtering work?

Tab BIG TRADES plots large options executions as color-coded markers directly on the Spot price chart — green for Call, red for Put. Two independent filters let you control the signal: Strike Proximity isolates trades within a chosen distance from the current spot (e.g. ≤15pt), focusing on ATM sweeps that force immediate dealer hedging. Dollar Notional and Contract Volume thresholds filter out smaller trades. Both filters are independent and adjustable in real time. The tab also shows Call Flow and Put Flow synchronized on the same time axis.

What does Tab FLOWS IV show and how does rendering perform at scale?

Tab FLOWS IV (Flow & IV) combines three synchronized panels: the SPX Spot price, 1-minute flow values (volume bars), and the ATM Implied Volatility Z-Score on a 30-minute rolling window — a normalized measure of how unusual the current IV level is relative to the intraday baseline. All seven tabs share a Universal Zoom Manager with auto-fit X/Y axes, double-click full-session reset, and a synchronized crosshair across all panels.

Performance is handled by a Peak-Preserving LOD Decimation engine (Numba-accelerated): on datasets above 3,000 points, the renderer reduces the series while guaranteeing that no spike or extreme tick is dropped. On real sessions with 500,000+ ticks, all panels render at 60 FPS without data loss at the crosshair level.
